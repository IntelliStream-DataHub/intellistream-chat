Why you shouldn't screenshot a secret

            
        

        

            
This page is built so a secret exists in as few places, for as short a time, as
                possible. It was encrypted in the sender's browser. The server only ever held
                ciphertext it had no key for, and forgot even that the moment you opened it. The
                link now works for nobody. And the text leaves this page after a few minutes, or
                as soon as you switch away.

            
A screenshot or a photo undoes all of that at once. It is an
                ordinary picture, and none of those protections apply to it:

            
    
                
  • It is backed up and synced. Phones and computers upload new
                    screenshots and photos to iCloud Photos, Google Photos, OneDrive or Dropbox on
                    their own, often to libraries shared with other devices or family members. The
                    secret now lives on servers you don't control, for as long as the photo does.
    • 
                
  • It is searchable. Photo apps and operating systems read the text
                    in images, so the password becomes something you — or anyone with your phone or
                    your cloud account — can find by searching for it.
    • 
                
  • It travels. Pictures get attached to tickets, pasted into chats
                    and forwarded, and stay in those histories indefinitely. Keeping secrets out of
                    chat history is exactly what this link was for.
    • 
                
  • It is hard to really delete. Deleted photos sit in a "recently
                    deleted" album for weeks, and in backups for longer.
    • 
                
  • A photo of the screen is no better. It lands in the same camera
                    roll, with the same syncing, and it can end up in the background of pictures you
                    share.
    • 
            

            
What to do instead

            
    
                
  • Copy the secret straight to where it belongs: your password manager, the login
                    form, the configuration file.
    • 
                
  • If you need to keep it, keep it in a password manager, not in a picture or a
                    note.
    • 
                
  • Clipboard history (Windows' Win+V, clipboard managers, a
                    synced clipboard between devices) also keeps copies; clear it when you're
                    done.
    • 
                
  • If you already took a picture, delete it, empty "recently deleted" as well, and
                    tell the person who shared it. The safe fix is to change the secret.
    • 
            

            
No web page can stop a screenshot or a camera. This one relies on
                you.
